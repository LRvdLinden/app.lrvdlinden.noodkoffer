WHAT THE APP DOES
Noodpakket ("emergency kit") helps households in the Netherlands build and maintain a 72-hour emergency kit, following the public advice of the Dutch government (denkvooruit.nl). It calculates what a household needs, tracks expiry dates, creates a shopping list, stores a printable emergency plan with important phone numbers, and can share one kit within a household. The app is in Dutch and English and works on iPhone and iPad.

ACCOUNT (OPTIONAL)
Every feature works without an account. An account is only needed to share one kit with household members. Demo credentials are in the Sign-in fields of App Review Information; the demo account is already confirmed and gets its own household on first sign-in.
Sign in: onboarding page "Together with your household" or Settings → Account and household → Sign in. There you see the household and its members, can invite another email address or delete the account. Changes to the kit and plan then sync between devices of the household.

HOW TO TEST
1. Launch the app and go through the onboarding: household size (e.g. 2 adults, 1 child), postcode (e.g. 7311 LG), reminders, optional account, optional Face ID lock. Every step can be skipped.
2. Tab "Kit": checklist with progress ring. Tap an item to change the quantity, set an expiry date or mark it "Not needed".
3. Scanning: tap the scan icon (top right of the Kit tab). Use the attached demo sheet (Noodpakket-review-barcodes): Spa Reine water and Varta AAA batteries are recognised and filled in; the DataMatrix code also fills in the expiry date 31-12-2027; "Scan date from packaging" reads the printed date. The camera is requested only at this point. Without a camera, type 5410013108009.
4. Tab "Expiry": items sorted by expiry date.
5. Tab "Shopping": everything still missing. Tick an item to add it to the kit.
6. Tab "Plan": meeting points, medical info and "Important numbers": national numbers (112, police, etc.), local numbers for the entered postcode (municipality, regional emergency radio station, water company) and own contacts (GP, family). Share button: print or save as PDF, including a one-page card for the meter cupboard. "What to do when..." shows offline guidance.
7. Tab "Settings": household, number of days, reminders, Messages from Noodpakket, Face ID lock, account, more apps by the developer.
8. Add the "Noodpakket" widget from the Home Screen widget gallery.

REGION / DATA NOTES
The checklist follows Dutch advice but the app works everywhere. Local numbers only exist for Dutch postcodes; elsewhere that card can be left empty. Unknown barcodes open a short form to fill in once; this is expected.

DATA SOURCES
Product data: public APIs of Open Food Facts and Open Products Facts (ODbL, attributed in the app); only the barcode is sent. Local numbers: the developer's server, using PDOK Locatieserver (Dutch government open data) and the open list of municipalities from organisaties.overheid.nl; only the postcode is sent. The app is independent and not affiliated with the Dutch government or the Denk Vooruit campaign.

PERMISSIONS
- Camera: only when the user taps scan. Processed on device.
- Notifications: local expiry and half-yearly check reminders, and (opt-in) Messages from Noodpakket.
- Face ID: only when the user turns on the app lock.
The app works fully when any of these is declined.

SERVERS AND SYNC
Without an account, data stays on the device and in the user's private iCloud database (CloudKit). With an account, the kit and plan of the household are stored on the developer's server (Cloudflare, EU) to share them; the account and its data can be deleted in the app. Messages from Noodpakket (off by default) stores only the push token, a random device ID and the app language.

IAP / ADS
No in-app purchases, subscriptions or ads.
